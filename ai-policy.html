AI Policy

What is allowed, what is required, and what has to be yours

Home Problem Sets Lectures AI Policy

Every problem carries a tag. The tag governs that problem, not the set.

The three tags

TagWhat it means
AI Required You are expected to use an AI to do the work. Paste a clean record of your final prompts and the AI's output into your answer.
AI Allowed Use one if you want to. The same record-keeping applies wherever you do.
No AI Do it by hand. A calculator or spreadsheet for the arithmetic is always fine — the ban is on outsourcing the reasoning, not on doing the multiplication. Where a problem names routine summary statistics, you may still have an AI produce those.

Answers without supporting work receive no credit. That applies to AI-produced numbers as much as to your own: if the output is not visible in what you submit, it cannot be graded.

The checking bonus (+2)

Once per submission you can earn 2 points on top of the 100-point scale for checks you initiate on an AI's output. A qualifying check is something like:

  • recomputing one of its numbers yourself;
  • cross-checking a figure against an outside source;
  • catching and correcting an error in what it produced;
  • or something else you dream up.

The check and its result have to be visible in what you submit. A companion file you upload alongside the PDF counts, as long as your answer points to it. Checks that a question itself requires do not qualify — the bonus is for the ones you thought of.

PS2 raises the bar to two distinct checks. PS1 required one. One qualifying check on PS2 earns no bonus, though it will be noted as a near miss in your feedback.

Working with other people

You may discuss approaches with your Learning Team, with Jacob, and with me. Each student completes and submits their own work.

A note on how to prompt

Work one problem at a time rather than pasting a whole assignment into a model and asking it to go. The sets are built so that the parts depend on each other; a model given the entire page at once tends to produce something fluent, unverifiable, and wrong in a way that is expensive to unwind. Ask for one step, look at what came back, then ask for the next.

And look at your data. Open the file, ask for the first few rows, plot something. Most of the costly errors in this course start with an analysis run on a dataset nobody examined.
